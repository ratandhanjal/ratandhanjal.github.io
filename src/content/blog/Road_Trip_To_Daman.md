There is something special about a road trip where the destination is only part of the adventure. The real experience lies in the roads you travel, the places you discover, the unexpected detours, the food you taste, and the memories you collect along the way.

My road trip from Noida to Daman and back was a journey of approximately **3,200 kilometres**, all driven in my own car.

Over several days, I travelled across **Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat** and back to Uttar Pradesh, passing through highways, villages, historic towns, mountain roads and coastal landscapes.

The journey included **Neemuch, Rampura, Indore, Shahada, Toranmal, Prakasha, Shirdi and Daman**, with every destination offering something completely different.

The journey began on **24 September at 6:00 AM** from Noida, and the adventure continued until I finally returned home on **3 October**.

---

## 3,200 Kilometres of Roads, Stories and Memories

Driving approximately 3,200 km in my own car made this trip more than just a vacation. It was a proper road adventure.

There were long highway stretches, early-morning departures, mountain roads, city traffic, village roads, food stops, sightseeing detours and late-night drives.

Instead of simply flying from one destination to another, I experienced the **gradual transformation of the landscape**.

The familiar roads around Noida slowly gave way to the landscapes of Rajasthan. Then came the historic towns and countryside of Madhya Pradesh, the mountains of Maharashtra, the spiritual atmosphere of Shirdi and finally the beaches and Portuguese heritage of Daman.

And then, after reaching the coast, I had to turn around and drive those thousands of kilometres back home.

That is what made the journey special.

> The destination was Daman, but the real adventure was the 3,200-kilometre road between home and the sea.

## Day 1 – Noida to Neemuch | 24 September

I started my journey from Noida at around **6:00 AM**.

The first stretch took me through Faridabad and the Palwal bypass, after which I joined the **Delhi-Mumbai Expressway**. I continued towards Sawai Madhopur, then travelled through Unaira, Hindoli, Bundi and Chittorgarh, finally reaching **Neemuch at around 6:00 PM**.

It was a long first day behind the wheel, but the changing landscape made the drive enjoyable.

After covering a significant distance, I checked into a hotel in Neemuch and stayed there for the night.

The next day, the journey moved away from the main highway and into history.

---

## Day 2 – Rampura: History Beyond the Highway | 25 September

The next morning, I drove around **50–60 km from Neemuch to Rampura**.

Rampura was an interesting discovery because it combined history, old architecture, rural life and the natural landscape around the nearby hills.

I explored the **remains of Rampura Fort**, saw the historic ring wall and followed a track towards the nearby mountains.

Rampura's history is traditionally associated with **Raja Rama Bhil**, who is regarded as the founder and first king of Rampura. His brother, Raja Bhana Bhil, is traditionally associated with the founding of nearby Bhanpura.

Later, control of the region passed to the **Chandrawat clan of Rajputs**, an offshoot of the Sisodia clan of Mewar.

Standing among the old walls and fort remains made the history feel much more real.

![Rampura Fort — the ancient palace complex and fort ruins nestled beneath the green hills of Madhya Pradesh](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/rampura_fort_1.jpg)

After exploring Rampura, I drove back towards Neemuch — and on the way back, I made one more stop that turned out to be one of the most spiritually significant moments of the entire journey.

### Bhadwa Mata Mandir, Neemuch — A Divine Detour

On the return from Rampura, I visited **Bhadwa Mata Mandir**, located approximately 18 km from Neemuch.

This was not a planned stop, but it became one of the most memorable moments of the trip.

The moment I arrived at the temple, there was an unmistakable sense of calm and reverence in the air.

![Bhadwa Mata Mandir — the golden-spired temple complex at Neemuch, one of the most revered shrines in Madhya Pradesh](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/Bhadwamata_temple_Neemuch.jpg)

#### Why Bhadwa Mata Mandir Is Famous

Bhadwa Mata Mandir is one of the most important pilgrimage sites in the **Malwa-Nimar region** of Madhya Pradesh. Here is why it holds such significance:

- **Ancient Origins:** The temple is estimated to be between **800 to 900 years old**, with its roots traced back to the 10th–11th century CE during the reign of the Parmar dynasty.

- **The Vaishno Devi of Malwa:** The temple is widely known as the **"Vaishno Devi of Malwa"**, drawing parallels with the famous Vaishno Devi shrine in Jammu and Kashmir. Devotees from across Madhya Pradesh and beyond make the pilgrimage here seeking blessings and healing.

- **Famous for Healing:** The temple has an extraordinary reputation as a **healing centre**. Devotees suffering from serious ailments — including paralysis, skin diseases and blindness — travel from across India in faith and hope. Many have reported miraculous recoveries after praying here.

- **The Sacred Baawdi (Stepwell):** One of the most remarkable features of the temple is its holy *Baawdi* — an ancient stepwell whose water is believed by devotees to possess **curative powers**, particularly for paralysis and skin-related ailments.

- **Akhand Jyoti — The Eternal Flame:** Inside the temple, an *Akhand Jyoti* — an eternal flame — has been kept burning continuously for many years without interruption. This flame is considered a living testament to the goddess's presence and divine power.

- **The Presiding Deity:** The main idol is **Mahamaya Bhadwa Mata**, seated on a large silver throne. She is surrounded by the nine Navdurgas, along with idols of Lord Ganesha, Lord Shiva, Goddess Parvati and a Sheshshai form of Lord Vishnu.

- **Grand Festivals:** The temple sees enormous gatherings during **Navratri** and the Hindu New Year (Gudi Padwa), when large fairs and religious events are held.

> Standing in the temple courtyard before continuing my long journey, I felt a quiet sense of peace — as though the road ahead had already been blessed.

After visiting the mandir, I returned to Neemuch and stayed there for the night.

## Day 3 – Neemuch to Indore | 26 September

On 26 September, I left Neemuch and continued towards Indore.

The route took me through **Jaora, Ratlam, Badnawar and Betma** before reaching Indore.

In the evening, I visited **Rajwada**, one of Indore's most recognizable historic landmarks.

![Rajwada Palace, Indore — the magnificent seven-storey Maratha architectural masterpiece of the Holkar dynasty](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/indore_rajwada_1.jpg)

### Food Hunting at Sarafa Bazaar

But the highlight of the night was undoubtedly **Sarafa Bazaar**.

At night, Sarafa Bazaar transforms into a famous street-food destination — what is a jewellery market by day becomes one of India's most vibrant food streets after dark.

I spent the evening exploring the market and trying different local dishes and specialities, including iconic Indore staples like *Bhutte ka Kees*, *Garadu*, *Shikanji* and *Poha-Jalebi*.

After a long day on the road, walking through the lively food streets of Indore was a perfect way to relax.

---

## Day 4 – 56 Dukan and the Road to Shahada | 27 September

The next morning, I visited **56 Dukan**, another famous food destination in Indore.

With numerous food shops concentrated in one area, it was another opportunity to experience Indore's legendary food culture.

After enjoying the food, I got back into my car and started the next major drive — **Indore to Shahada, Maharashtra**.

After reaching Shahada, I checked into a hotel and stayed there for the night.

---

## Day 5 – Toranmal | 28 September

The next morning, I drove from Shahada towards **Toranmal**, one of the most scenic destinations of the trip.

Toranmal is a **hill station in the Satpura mountain range** of Maharashtra, situated at approximately **1,150 metres above sea level**.

The landscape changed dramatically as I climbed into the hills. The long highway stretches were replaced by winding mountain roads, forests, viewpoints and greenery.

![Toranmal — a spectacular waterfall cascades down the forested Satpura cliffs during the monsoon season](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/toranmal_hills.webp)

Toranmal is known for its natural beauty, including places such as:

- **Yashwant Lake**
- **Lotus Lake**
- **Satpura Viewpoint**
- **Nagarjun Point**
- **Khadki Point**

The region also has strong religious and tribal connections, including the **Gorakhnath Temple** and the annual Mahashivratri gathering.

![Toranmal viewpoint — the rolling hills and deep valleys of the Satpura plateau stretching to the horizon](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/toranmal_hills_1.jpg)

I spent the day exploring several places around Toranmal before driving back to Shahada for the night.

After several days of driving through highways and plains, the mountain roads of Toranmal were one of the **highlights of the entire 3,200-km journey**.

### A Little About Shahada

Shahada, in Maharashtra's **Nandurbar district**, became an important stop on my journey because of its location near Toranmal and Prakasha.

The region is part of the broader Khandesh area and has strong connections with the Tapi River, agriculture and tribal culture.

For me, Shahada was more than just an overnight halt. It became the **base from which I explored two very different sides of the region** — the mountains of Toranmal and the religious heritage of Prakasha.

---

## Day 6 – Prakasha and Kedareshwar Temple | 29 September

On 29 September, I drove from Shahada to **Prakasha**, situated along the Tapi River.

Prakasha is popularly known as **Dakshin Kashi**, or the *"Kashi of the South"*, because of its religious importance and ancient temples.

I visited **Kedareshwar Temple** and explored the surrounding area.

![Prakasha — the sacred Kedareshwar Temple complex, revered as Dakshin Kashi — the Kashi of the South](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/prakasha_temple_1.jpg)

The atmosphere was completely different from Toranmal. The previous day had been about mountains, forests and viewpoints; this day was about **temples, rivers and spirituality**.

After visiting Prakasha, I returned to Shahada for the night.

---

## Day 7 – Shahada to Shirdi to Daman | 30 September

On 30 September, I started another long driving day.

I left Shahada and travelled towards **Shirdi**.

After reaching Shirdi, I visited the famous **Sai Baba Temple** and had darshan.

### Why Shirdi Is Famous

Shirdi is inseparable from **Sai Baba of Shirdi**, one of India's most widely revered spiritual figures.

Sai Baba spent much of his life in Shirdi, where his teachings emphasized **faith, patience, compassion and harmony**.

He lived at Dwarkamai, while Gurusthan is associated with his early presence in Shirdi.

Sai Baba attained **Maha-Samadhi on 15 October 1918**, and his resting place is now the famous Samadhi Mandir.

For millions of devotees, Shirdi is not simply a tourist destination. It is an **important pilgrimage centre and a place of faith**.

![Shirdi Sai Baba Samadhi Mandir — the divine inner sanctum of the Samadhi Mandir adorned in gold, with the sacred idol of Sai Baba](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/shirdi_temple.jpg)

For me, having darshan at the temple was one of the most meaningful moments of the trip.

But there was still a long road ahead.

After visiting Shirdi, I got back into my car and continued towards Daman, travelling through **Nashik, Peint and Nana Pondha**.

By the time I reached Daman, the journey had taken me from the mountains and temples to the coast.

---

## Daman – Finally Reaching the Sea

After days of driving through inland India, **seeing the sea felt like a reward**.

I checked into my hotel and went out at night to explore **Devka Beach and Fort Road**.

Daman was very different from every destination I had visited before.

It had beaches, forts, churches, old streets and a strong **Portuguese influence**.

### Daman's Portuguese History

Daman has a fascinating colonial history.

The Portuguese established control over Daman in the **16th century**, and Portuguese rule continued until **1961**, when the territory became part of India.

This long Portuguese presence can still be seen in the city's architecture, forts and churches.

### Moti Daman Fort

One of Daman's most important historical landmarks is **Moti Daman Fort**.

The fort was constructed during Portuguese rule and became an important defensive structure.

Walking around the fort provides a glimpse into Daman's colonial past, with historic churches and other Portuguese-era structures within the fortified area.

![Moti Daman Fort at night — the ancient Portuguese fort walls dramatically lit up, with the crescent moon rising above](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/moti_daman_fort_1.jpg)

![Moti Daman Fort Road at dusk — the fort's golden walls glowing at sunset, stretching along the Fort Road promenade](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/moti_daman_fort_2.jpg)

![The historic gateway of Moti Daman Fort — the Portuguese stone archway with its royal coat of arms still visible after centuries](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/moti_daman_fort_3.jpg)

### Nani Daman

Across the Damanganga River lies **Nani Daman**, another important part of the city.

The **Fort of St. Jerome** and the **Church of Our Lady of the Sea** are among the area's notable landmarks.

This mixture of old fortifications, churches, river views and the Arabian Sea gives Daman a **unique character**.

## Day 8 – Exploring Daman | 1 October

The next day was dedicated to exploring Daman.

We visited:

- **Jambora Beach**
- **Nani Daman**
- **Mini Goa**
- **Daman Fort**
- **Devka Beach**
- **Lighthouse**

One of my favourite experiences was spending time at the beach and watching the changing sea.

At around 1:00 PM, we saw the water come close to the beach. Later, it receded back towards the ocean.

We enjoyed the beach, had lunch, returned to the hotel for a bath and some rest, and then came back again.

![Daman Beach — the beautiful Devka beach promenade with coconut palms and the Arabian Sea coastline stretching into the distance](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/daman_beach_0.jpg)

The evening turned into a long beach session, and we enjoyed ourselves until late at night.

After thousands of kilometres of driving, it was nice to simply **sit back and enjoy the sea**.

---

## Day 9 – Jambora Beach and the Return Journey | 2 October

On 2 October, I woke up early and went to **Jambora Beach at around 6:00 AM**.

The morning was peaceful, and it was a great way to begin the final day in Daman.

![Jampore Beach Road, Daman — the serene early-morning beach road lined with casuarina trees, with the calm Arabian Sea to the right](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/jampore_beach.jpg)

Then it was time to start the long drive home.

I began the return journey towards Noida, travelling through **Surat and Vadodara** before joining the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway.

I continued towards **Kota**, where I stopped for the night.

Another long day behind the wheel was complete.

---

## Day 10 – Kota to Noida | 3 October

On 3 October, I started the final leg from **Kota to Noida**.

I continued along the highway and eventually reached home.

After approximately **3,200 kilometres of driving**, the road trip had finally come to an end.

No story of this road trip would be complete without a special mention of the one who made it all possible.

My car.

For **10 days and 3,200 kilometres**, it never let me down.

It climbed the mountain roads of Toranmal without a complaint. It powered through the long expressway stretches of Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh without hesitation. It handled the coastal humidity of Daman, the city traffic of Indore and the village roads of Rampura — all with the same calm reliability.

Early morning starts at 6:00 AM. Late-night arrivals. Mountain curves. Potholed village roads. Highway highways at full speed. The car took everything in its stride.

![A road tripper's best friend — 3,200 km, 10 days, and not a single breakdown](/blog_images/daman_road_trip/Thanks_to_my_car.jpg)

Over the ten days, the car was the one constant through all the changing landscapes — from the dry plains of Rajasthan to the cool hills of Maharashtra to the salty air of the Arabian coast.

Every kilometre was clocked in that cabin. Every morning it was ready when I was. Every evening it brought me safely to the next destination.

**Thank you, for being the quiet hero of this 3,200-kilometre adventure.**
